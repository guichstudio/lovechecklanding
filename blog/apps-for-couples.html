10 Apps for Couples: Find Your Best Match

Updated August 28, 2026

You want to feel closer, but the timing rarely lines up. One of you is ready for a meaningful conversation while the other is exhausted after work. Long-distance couples may have affection to share but no easy moment to share it. Even couples living together can spend an entire evening handling chores, messages, and logistics without asking how the other person is really doing.

The best apps for couples don't try to replace face-to-face time. They make small moments easier to start, especially when conversation prompts, shared memories, intimacy exercises, or money discussions keep getting postponed. This roundup compares tools by the couple problem they solve, the routine they create, their practical limitations, and the relationship style they suit best.

Digital relationship tools have a growing evidence base. A 2025 systematic review found that 15 eligible studies generally improved relationship satisfaction, while earlier clinical trials included nearly 5,000 participants in total (review summary and source links). That doesn't mean every app works for every couple, and these products are relationship-wellness tools, not therapy or medical services. If you're dealing with abuse, addiction, betrayal, or a mental-health crisis, consult a licensed professional.

Use a simple test: choose the problem first, check whether both partners are willing to participate, compare friction and cost, then try the routine for a week. The app that fits your actual evenings is usually more useful than the one with the longest feature list.

1. LoveCheck

LoveCheck builds connection through small daily rituals and gives couples a shared reason to check in. Its Daily Love Quests, including Memory Flash, Song of the Day, and Mood of the Day, prompt each partner to respond and maintain a rhythm without requiring a long conversation.

The app adds light game mechanics through Love Points, levels, streaks, and a Days Together Counter. The Love Calendar records completed and missed days, while Love Doodles let partners send quick hand-drawn messages when neither has time for a full exchange. A Home Screen widget keeps the relationship visible without creating another planning task.

What makes the routine work

LoveCheck fits newly dating couples forming habits, busy partners who prefer low-effort prompts, and long-distance couples who want more variety than another “miss you” text. The Couple Quiz offers Classic and Spicy modes, allowing partners to choose a comfortable starting point. A compatibility test takes approximately three minutes, giving couples a prompt to discuss similarities and differences rather than treating the result as a verdict.

The app is available through the LoveCheck website, with versions on the App Store and Google Play. Cross-platform access helps when partners use different devices. One Premium subscription covers both linked accounts, reducing payment coordination and keeping the same features available to each partner. LoveCheck also documents privacy controls and provides editorial resources with exercises and conversation starters.

Practical rule: Choose one Daily Love Quest and attach it to a predictable moment, such as after dinner. Let the rest of the features stay optional.

The main trade-off is that the full experience requires Premium, while the website does not list a specific subscription rate. Shared progress also depends on participation from both partners. If one person stops opening the app, streaks and milestones carry less meaning. LoveCheck reports a 4.9 average rating and use by over 12K couples, but those figures come from the product's own published information, so use them as background rather than a direct comparison with older apps.

Best for: Couples who want playful daily connection, mixed-device access, milestone tracking, and short activities they can repeat without much setup.

2. Paired

Paired gives busy couples guided daily questions, quizzes, expert tips, mini-courses, and light games on communication, sex, and money. Partners can answer separately before comparing responses, which brings different assumptions into the open and gives the conversation a clear starting point.

A practical routine is easy to set up. Open the app during a commute, after dinner, or before bed, answer one prompt, and discuss it while the topic is fresh. A couple who enjoys conversation cards can vary the format with quizzes and games, while partners with limited time can stick to shorter activities. If you want extra prompts away from the app, pair its questions with these questions to feel closer to your partner.

The Paired app is available on iOS and Android, and one Premium subscription includes the partner. Product information for this comparison lists a $14.99 monthly Premium option in the United States. Check the current subscription terms before starting, particularly if you are weighing Paired against free-first tools or apps with different payment structures.

The format works best when both partners answer and treat differences as conversation material rather than a scorecard. Its main limitation is depth. The mini-lessons can prompt useful discussions, but couples seeking intensive repair work or therapy-level support may find them too light. Much of the richer content requires Premium, so the free version may provide only a partial view of the experience.

Best for: Busy couples who want frequent, guided check-ins, varied topics, and a routine that fits around short pockets of time.

3. Lasting

Lasting offers a structured relationship curriculum with guided plans, assessments, lessons, exercises, and on-demand couples retreats led by licensed therapists. This format suits partners who agree that an issue needs attention but want a clear place to start.

A couple can select a communication plan, complete an assessment, and follow the recommended material together. Partners who prefer concentrated learning can choose a retreat without arranging travel. The sequence creates a roadmap, which may work better than open-ended question decks for couples who struggle to maintain momentum after a promising conversation.

The Lasting relationship program is available on iOS and Android. Its strength lies in organization and progression. Each topic comes with an exercise and a next step, so couples do not have to design every conversation themselves. A couple might set aside one evening each week, complete one activity, and discuss how it applies to a current disagreement or recurring habit.

Where Lasting can feel heavy

Lasting's guided approach can feel like homework for partners who prefer games, streaks, or quick prompts. It may also be harder to fit into a busy week when the goal is a light reconnection. Subscription pricing can change with promotions, so check the current terms before committing.

Couples who want additional practice at home can pair the program with these couple therapy exercises at home. Self-guided activities can support reflection and discussion, but serious relationship problems may require care from a licensed professional.

Best for: Couples who want a research-informed curriculum, assessments, and a clear path through common relationship topics.

4. Relish

Relish combines personalized lessons, progress tracking, and optional human coaching in a single subscription. It suits couples who want a guided routine but also need help applying an exercise to their own arguments, schedules, or communication habits.

A typical week might include one lesson, a short discussion, and a coaching exchange about putting the idea into practice. Progress tracking gives partners a place to revisit their goal, such as listening more carefully, and check whether the change is showing up during real conversations.

The Relish relationship app is available on iOS and Android. Its distinct advantage is the choice between working independently and requesting more personalized direction. That flexibility can help couples who start with self-guided material but want support when a recurring disagreement proves difficult to handle alone.

The trade-offs are practical. Pricing varies by market, and exact rates may not appear upfront. Coaching availability and response times can vary too, so check what the subscription includes before joining. Relish may feel too structured for partners who only want spontaneous date ideas. Couples seeking full therapy should confirm that the service matches their needs and consult a licensed professional when appropriate.

Use Relish by setting one weekly session and bringing one current relationship issue to the lesson or coaching conversation. That keeps the program connected to daily life instead of turning it into another unfinished course.

Coaching can help you apply an exercise, but it doesn't make an app a replacement for professional treatment.

Best for: Couples who want lessons, accountability, and optional coaching without beginning with full therapy.

5. Between

Between operates as a private communication and memory hub for couples who already have strong conversational habits. It brings private chat, voice calls, video features, photo and memory sharing, a shared calendar, and reminders into one couples-only space.

That setup helps partners who want relationship moments kept separate from general messaging. Long-distance couples can collect photos and messages in one place, while cohabiting partners can use the calendar for plans, anniversaries, and reminders. The pairing-code invitation flow keeps setup focused on connecting the two accounts.

The Between app is a mature choice for private communication. Its main strength is continuity. Partners can keep conversations, memories, and practical coordination together instead of spreading them across a messenger, camera roll, and calendar.

The trade-off is its limited guidance. Between supports communication, memory keeping, and planning, but it offers fewer relationship exercises than coaching-oriented apps. If you both keep defaulting to logistics, opening the app will not by itself create a deeper conversation. Parts of the experience also feel more utility-focused than growth-focused.

Try Between by using the calendar for shared plans and saving one meaningful photo or message each week. That routine makes its value visible quickly. Couples who want structured reflection or conflict prompts may need to pair it with another app.

Best for: Couples who prioritize private messaging, shared memories, calls, and calendar coordination.

6. Lovewick

Lovewick suits couples who want questions, date ideas, reminders, and memory keeping in a lightweight, free-first format. It brings question cards, crowdsourced date inspiration, a shared memories timeline, wishlists, bucket lists, and reminders for favorite ideas or important dates into one place.

On a Friday evening, one partner can save a date idea, choose a question, or add a future experience to the shared list. New couples can use the prompts to move beyond surface conversation. Long-term partners may get more value from the timeline and wishlists, which turn good intentions into plans they can revisit.

The Lovewick couples app keeps its core experience accessible, with optional low-cost Premium for additional content. That free-first approach makes it easy to try together and supports occasional use rather than a daily curriculum. Couples can open it when they want inspiration, then return to their normal routine.

Its practical limit is depth. Lovewick offers enjoyable prompts, but couples facing recurring conflict may need a tool or professional support focused on repair skills. Some advanced sets and milestones also require an upgrade, so the free library may provide only part of the available content.

Best for: Couples who want playful questions, date inspiration, memories, and a low-pressure free starting point.

7. Gottman Card Decks

Gottman Card Decks focuses exclusively on conversation cards and practices adapted from workshops associated with the Gottman Institute. Product information supplied for this comparison lists more than 14 card decks and more than 1,000 conversation cards. You can favorite and sort prompts, then return to the questions that led to useful discussions.

A couple can open the app for five minutes after dinner, choose a deck that suits their mood, and put the phone away when the conversation ends. The format suits partners who dislike streaks, scores, and gamification, or who want a focused supplement to therapy. It creates an occasional conversation ritual rather than a broader tracking routine.

The official Gottman Card Decks app page lists availability on iOS and Android. Its research-informed orientation gives the prompts more direction than random question generators, while the narrow focus keeps setup simple.

That focus also sets the app's practical limit. It has no calendar, journal, analytics, built-in partner comparisons, or shared progress features. Partners must choose when to use the cards and decide how to follow up afterward. Couples seeking playful activities can use dedicated games for couples online, but Gottman Card Decks works better when the goal is a purposeful conversation.

Best for: Couples who want free, evidence-informed conversation prompts, minimal setup, and a flexible five-minute discussion routine.

8. Love Nudge

Love Nudge turns the Five Love Languages framework into practical reminders. Partners complete the in-app quiz, view each other's profiles, set goals, and receive nudges linked to how they prefer to give and receive affection.

Its main value is specificity. If one partner wants to show care but is unsure what to do, the app offers an action connected to the other person's stated preference. That might mean an act of service, encouraging words, shared time, a gift, or physical touch. The couple gets a shared vocabulary and one small behavior to try.

A partner can open Love Nudge during a busy day, choose a manageable action, and follow up later. This creates a light habit around expressing affection, without requiring a long session or a detailed check-in.

The Love Nudge app is free on iOS and Android, and its accounts connect with the Five Love Languages platform. It is easy to test for couples who already use that framework.

Love Nudge focuses specifically on affectionate habits through the Five Love Languages framework. It does not cover conflict resolution, repair skills, financial discussions, or broad relationship assessments. Treat the quiz as a conversation aid rather than a fixed description of either partner. Preferences can change with context, so reminders should invite discussion instead of becoming another obligation.

Best for: Couples who want simple, personalized affection prompts and already like the Love Languages framework.

9. Coral

Coral concentrates on sexual wellness and intimacy education. It combines guided exercises, expert-written lessons, partner chat, and synced subscription settings for couples who want a private way to discuss desire, comfort, and intimacy.

Its main strength is giving difficult conversations a clear starting point. Partners who can discuss schedules and errands but avoid talking about sex can work through lessons or exercises at a mutually comfortable pace. Occasional live Date Night events add a shared activity for members who prefer more guidance than a static article.

A couple might open Coral for a short lesson, discuss one prompt, and decide whether to try an exercise. That routine keeps intimacy conversations specific, though it still requires both partners to participate willingly. No app can replace consent, patience, or a conversation that needs more personal support.

The Coral intimacy app is free to start, while Premium provides access to the full library. Its specialization helps couples find intimacy material without sorting through calendars, general messaging, or broad relationship curricula. Couples wanting one app for several relationship areas may find that narrow focus limiting, and they should review the available content and subscription terms before committing.

Consent and emotional safety come before completing any activity. Pause if either partner feels pressured. Concerns involving trauma, coercion, abuse, or a mental-health crisis require support beyond an app, such as a licensed professional.

Best for: Couples who want guided intimacy education, sexual communication practice, and private partner exercises.

10. Honeydue

Honeydue handles a relationship problem that connection-focused apps often leave aside: coordinating money without requiring complete financial visibility. Partners can link accounts, create shared budgets, set bill reminders, and discuss individual transactions or expenses in the app.

Its privacy controls suit couples who are still developing their money routine. You can share household information while keeping a transaction private, such as a surprise gift. That approach supports clearer conversations without making financial transparency an all-or-nothing choice.

The Honeydue finance app is free, with optional services. Honeydue works best alongside a general relationship app rather than serving as a standalone connection tool. Try it by listing recurring bills, assigning responsibility for each one, and using the transaction chat to clarify unfamiliar charges.

Bank connection reliability varies by institution, so confirm that your accounts connect properly before making the app part of your entire financial routine. It also lacks conversation prompts, intimacy exercises, and milestone tracking. Its focus is financial coordination, which suits couples who want practical help rather than relationship content.

Separate the tool from the tension: Use the transaction chat to clarify facts first, then schedule a calmer conversation if the issue involves values or trust.

Best for: Couples who need shared budgets, bill reminders, transaction conversations, and flexible financial privacy.

Choose the App That Matches Your Real Routine

The right choice depends less on the app with the most features and more on the problem you're both willing to address. If you want a playful daily ritual and mixed-device connection, LoveCheck is the natural starting point. Its Couple Quiz, Daily Love Quests, Love Calendar, and Days Together counter give you several ways to create a small shared habit without planning a full date.

Choose Paired if you want bite-sized guided check-ins across communication, sex, money, and other everyday topics. Choose Lasting if you prefer a structured curriculum with assessments, lessons, and guided programs. Relish is a better fit when coaching and accountability matter as much as the exercises themselves.

Your choice changes when the main problem is practical rather than reflective:

  • Private communication and memories: Between gives you a dedicated channel, shared photos, calls, and calendar tools.
  • Free-first questions and date ideas: Lovewick keeps the experience playful and accessible, with optional Premium content.
  • Low-friction conversation prompts: Gottman Card Decks lets you open a deck, talk for a few minutes, and stop.
  • Love-language actions: Love Nudge turns a familiar framework into reminders and specific gestures.
  • Intimacy practice: Coral focuses on sexual communication, education, and guided exercises.
  • Shared finances: Honeydue handles budgets, bills, account connections, and transaction conversations.

Platform access deserves a close look. Confirm that both partners can use the app on their phones, especially if you're comparing iOS and Android. Then read the subscription terms, identify what remains free, and check whether one subscription covers both linked accounts. LoveCheck currently offers one Premium subscription for the couple, while product pricing and availability can change, so verify current terms before subscribing. Privacy deserves the same attention. Review what the app stores, what partners can see, and how to delete an account.

Try the routine before committing

Run a seven-day test:

  1. Choose a reliable time: Pick a moment that already exists, such as after dinner or before bed.
  2. Complete one small activity: Answer one question, share a mood, review a bill, or save a date idea.
  3. Discuss the experience: Ask whether it felt natural, useful, awkward, or too demanding.
  4. Make a decision: Keep the app if both partners can imagine repeating it. Switch if the routine creates more friction than connection.

Couples don't need identical preferences. One partner may enjoy quizzes while the other prefers a shared calendar. The best compromise is an app routine that feels easy enough to repeat and meaningful enough to notice.

Apps can support connection, but they can't replace safety planning, licensed professional help, or direct action in serious situations involving abuse, addiction, betrayal, or a mental-health crisis. Use relationship-wellness tools for small habits, reflection, and shared activities. Ask for professional support when the problem needs more than a prompt or reminder.
